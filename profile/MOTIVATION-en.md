# Our Motivation

## PSOURCE was not planned. It grew.

PSOURCE did not start with a business plan, a marketing strategy or the ambition to build an ecosystem.

It started with a much simpler problem:

**I needed software that still worked.**

Over the years, I had built websites and complex WordPress Multisite projects using plugins and themes that had become an important part of those installations.

Then things started changing.

WordPress evolved. APIs changed. Dependencies became outdated. Developers abandoned projects. Plugins stopped being maintained or became incompatible with newer versions.

And suddenly, software that had worked perfectly for years became a problem.

For a small project, replacing a plugin might be easy.

For a large, established website or Multisite network that has grown over many years, it often isn't.

There may be thousands of pieces depending on each other. Rebuilding everything from scratch simply because the original developer stopped maintaining a plugin is not always a realistic option.

So I started learning how to maintain those projects myself.

I fixed things.

I modernized old code.

I replaced deprecated APIs.

I improved security.

I removed obsolete dependencies.

I adapted software to newer PHP and JavaScript versions.

And whenever something was missing, I added it.

Over time, some of those projects became heavily modified compared to their original versions. Some were merged into smaller suites. Some were completely rebuilt. And eventually, I started creating new software myself.

That is how PSOURCE grew.

---

## ClassicPress changed the direction

I have been using ClassicPress from the very beginning.

And I immediately liked what it represented.

While WordPress increasingly moved in a direction that made many of my existing projects harder to maintain, ClassicPress gave me a stable foundation where I could continue building without constantly having to redesign everything around the next major change.

I did not choose ClassicPress because I wanted to fight WordPress.

I chose it because **it was the right tool for the projects I was working on.**

And that distinction is important to me.

PSOURCE supports both ClassicPress and WordPress.

I don't believe in a "WordPress versus ClassicPress" war.

Different projects have different requirements, and developers should be able to choose the tool that fits the job.

ClassicPress simply gives us something increasingly valuable:

**stability, control and the freedom to build on a foundation without constantly wondering whether the next shiny feature will break what we already built.**

---

## More than individual plugins

Today, PSOURCE contains a large number of plugins, themes and development projects.

But looking at them individually doesn't always tell the whole story.

Many of them were created to work together.

One plugin may provide the foundation while another extends it. A third may add functionality to both. Several projects together can become a much more powerful solution than any of them could provide alone.

This is particularly important for complex environments such as Multisite networks, communities and e-commerce systems.

PSOURCE therefore isn't just a collection of plugins.

It is becoming an **ecosystem**.

---

## Why Open Source matters

I believe good software should be allowed to outlive its original developer.

If someone creates something useful and eventually no longer has the time or resources to maintain it, that shouldn't automatically mean the software has to disappear.

Someone else can pick it up.

Someone can modernize it.

Someone can translate it.

Someone can improve it.

Someone can take the original idea and build something completely different from it.

That's one of the things I love about Open Source.

And that's exactly what happened with many of the projects that eventually became part of PSOURCE.

---

## From necessity to contribution

For many years, most of my time went into simply keeping everything running and improving the software for my own projects.

I maintain a large number of plugins and themes, and there was simply not much time left to build a community around them.

That is changing.

The projects are now reaching a point where I can start looking beyond my own installations.

And that brings me to ClassicPress.

I believe ClassicPress has enormous potential, but a CMS needs more than a good core.

It needs an ecosystem.

It needs developers.

It needs people who build plugins and themes.

It needs testers.

It needs people reporting bugs.

It needs translators.

It needs documentation.

It needs people who take an existing idea and make it better.

And most importantly, it needs people who are willing to build things **for the long term**.

That is where I want PSOURCE to contribute.

Not by replacing existing projects.

Not by claiming that there is only one correct solution.

And certainly not by trying to tell ClassicPress what it should become.

I simply want to bring what I have built over the last years into the wider community and see what can happen when other people can build on it too.

---

## There is room for more than one solution

I think this is especially important for the ClassicPress ecosystem.

Someone uses ClassicCommerce and it does everything they need?

**Fantastic.**

Someone is running a complex Multisite shop network and needs something different?

**Maybe PSOURCE MarketPress is the right tool.**

Someone wants to extend ClassicCommerce to solve that particular problem?

**Even better.**

Someone finds an old Open Source project and gives it a new life?

**That's great too.**

A healthy ecosystem does not need one perfect solution.

It needs **choice**.

It needs enough developers and enough ideas that people can choose the right tool for the job.

---

## Control matters

There is another reason why I believe ClassicPress has an opportunity right now.

People are increasingly surrounded by software that wants to make decisions for them.

AI is being pushed into almost everything.

Cloud services are becoming mandatory for things that used to work locally.

Subscriptions are replacing software ownership.

Data is constantly being collected and sent somewhere.

And users increasingly have less control over the software they depend on.

I think there is value in doing things differently.

Not because every new technology is bad.

Not because AI or cloud services have no legitimate uses.

But because **choice and control matter**.

A CMS should be a tool.

It should help people create something.

It should not dictate how they work.

And the software running your website should ultimately remain something you can understand, control and build upon.

---

## What comes next?

I don't have a grand master plan for PSOURCE.

I'm one person.

I've spent years maintaining and modernizing these projects, often simply because I needed them myself.

Now I'm finally at a point where I can open that work up more deliberately and see where it goes.

Some projects may be useful to ClassicPress users.

Some may not.

Some may eventually find their way into the ClassicPress Directory.

Others may remain primarily useful for WordPress or for specialized projects.

That's okay.

If someone finds a PSOURCE project useful, uses it, reports a bug, suggests an improvement, translates it, submits code or builds something on top of it, then the project has already achieved something.

And if someone takes one of my projects and turns it into something completely different?

**That's Open Source.**

I don't need to be the person who builds everything.

I'd much rather be the person who helps create a foundation that allows other people to build something too.

---

## Let's see where this goes

PSOURCE started because I didn't want useful software to disappear.

It grew because I kept finding things worth saving.

And now I would like to see what happens when those things become part of a larger community.

Maybe PSOURCE can help fill some gaps in the ClassicPress ecosystem.

Maybe other developers will improve what I've started.

Maybe someone will build something completely new on top of it.

Maybe we'll discover ideas I haven't even thought about yet.

I don't know.

And that's actually the exciting part.

**I built PSOURCE because I needed these tools.**

**Now I'd like to see what happens when other people can build on them too.**
